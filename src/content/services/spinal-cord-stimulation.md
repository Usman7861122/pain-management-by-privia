A spinal cord stimulator delivers mild electrical pulses that interrupt abnormal pain signals as they travel through the spinal cord to the brain. These devices have been used since the 1970s and have become safer and more effective over time.

After nerve damage, pain pathways can "short-circuit," so normal touch feels painful. Some medicines dull this pain, but none has been shown to work as well as stimulation, which aims to bring nerves back to normal or near-normal behavior. Dr. Stuart Hough and Dr. Ramani Peruvemba each have more than 20 years of experience with it.
